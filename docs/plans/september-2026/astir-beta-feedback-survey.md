## Collection and follow-up process

1. Before the first cohort: name a research owner, pilot the form with two people, time it, test every response field, and verify the linked response sheet and its access. Keep automatic email collection off. Do not show response summaries to respondents.
2. Invitation: the owner records tester ID, app build and invite date. Send the survey after a meaningful session or a failed attempt to get started, ideally within 24–48 hours. Invite delivery remains a team action.
3. Reminder: if there is no response after three days, send one personal reminder. Record declines without chasing.
4. Daily collection: review new responses once each workday. Mark access blocker, bug, usability problem, missing capability or positive observation. Keep raw quotes separate from the team’s interpretation.
5. Follow-up: offer a 20-minute meeting to respondents who opted in. Prioritize blocked users and include successful users. Proposal: 8–10 meetings across the 30 friends, with capacity reviewed after the first two.
6. Meeting agenda: 2 minutes for context, 10 minutes to walk through the user’s own goal, 5 minutes to probe one or two survey answers, and 3 minutes for the top change and next steps. Ask permission separately before recording.
7. Issue triage: reproduce problems when possible. Each actionable finding gets a concise problem, build/context, evidence, frequency, impact, owner and next step. Link a Linear issue when the team chooses to act.
8. Weekly review: review themes, counts with denominators, release blockers and fixes. A survey or meeting is not a prerequisite to count a friend with working app access toward 30.
9. Close the loop: tell the tester what changed and, where useful, ask them to retry. Track retest outcomes instead of treating an issue’s closure as proof of a better experience.

### Tracker fields

Tester ID; contact provided voluntarily; invite owner; build/device; first-use date; survey sent; response received; follow-up permission; meeting offered/booked/completed; key finding; severity; issue link; owner; fix state; retest result.

### Form setup

- Thirty questions on one continuous form page; this review copy groups them into six topics. Seven required questions, all remaining questions optional.
- Multiple choice for experiences and outcomes, with “not tried” or equivalent options where needed.
- True/false-style questions include an uncertainty or not-tried option.
- No quiz scoring, file uploads or automatic newsletter enrollment.
- Google Forms publishes only after the owner reviews the draft and access settings.
- Publication and distribution are separate. No invitations or meeting requests have been sent as part of preparing this survey.
