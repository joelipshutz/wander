## Problem Statement

Discover currently assumes a social graph before it helps create one. The Places mode can show privacy-filtered, newest-first activity from followed people, but the Members mode is primarily a search box plus people the user already follows. A user who does not know another member's handle receives little proactive help, and a user who sees a profile gets little explanation of what following that person will add to their map.

The product hypothesis is that a Beli-inspired, two-mode Discover surface can close this loop:

1. Keep rec.me place-first by opening on **Places**.
2. Preserve **Activity** as a newest-first view of followed people's visible place saves.
3. Add a conditional **Places You May Have Been** or **Pick Up Where You Left Off** shelf as a low-friction memory-recovery surface, with the title determined by evidence strength.
4. Turn **People** into a proactive follow surface that explains why each person is shown and previews the place value following them will unlock.

The primary outcome is not more browsing. It is more useful follow edges, which should make follow-powered activity and place discovery better on the next visit.

## Demand Evidence

There is no direct tester evidence yet that proactive people recommendations will fix Discover. Joe selected: “No direct signal yet; this is a product hypothesis based on the current UX and Beli.”

Evidence available today is product and implementation evidence, not demand validation:

- Current Members discovery requires a user to search for a name or handle or browse people they already follow.
- The existing empty Activity state tells users to follow more people but does not provide the follow action in context.
- The Beli references demonstrate a legible two-mode model: restaurant/place recovery in one mode and proactive member discovery in another.
- rec.me's north star depends on network density: trusted people's saved places become the useful discovery layer only after the user follows enough relevant people.

Because this is a hypothesis, the feature must ship as an instrumented experiment. It must not be treated as validated merely because the UI is more complete.

## Status Quo

Today a low-network user opens Discover and sees:

- **Places:** natural-language search plus up to ten newest-first visible saves from followed users.
- **Members:** member search results, then a list of profiles the user already follows.
- **Follow value:** mostly deferred until a profile is opened or new activity appears later.
- **Cold start:** an empty or thin Activity section and no proactive path from that empty state to useful people.
- **Place recovery:** no dedicated surface for likely visited-but-unsaved places.

The practical workaround is knowing and typing another member's name or handle. That is weak for testers joining a small network and impossible for users who do not yet know who is on rec.me.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The first target is an authenticated iPhone user who follows 0–4 non-blocked accounts, so Discover is too thin to feel useful.

The narrowest useful wedge is one balanced Discover screen with two modes:

### Places, default mode

1. Search.
2. Activity, newest first.
3. Conditional place-recovery shelf, titled from evidence strength.

### People

1. Search by name or handle.
2. Suggested for You.
3. More in Your City, using the user's explicit profile city rather than device location.
4. Inline Follow/Following actions with an immediate explanation of why the person is relevant, public profile context, and explicit copy describing the place access a follow can unlock.

The first production recommendation blend is deliberately narrower than the long-term blend. It is ordered by trust and relevance:

1. People who follow the user whom the user does not follow back.
2. People followed by at least one account the user already follows.
3. Curated, real profiles with recommendation consent and curated-fallback eligibility in the same explicit profile city.
4. Curated, real profiles with the same consent/eligibility but without a city match as the final fallback.

Native Contacts is excluded from REC-90 v1 because it has not shipped. Coarse device location is also excluded from people ranking. Both can enter a later recommendation-source experiment without changing the card contract.

Fictional profiles are allowed only in local, preview, automated-test, or clearly isolated staging data. Production recommendations must represent real accounts and must never fabricate followers, activity, ratings, or social proof.

## Release Slices

REC-90 contains two product loops, but they must not be treated as one experiment or one required implementation batch.

### Slice 0: clickable/prototype validation

- Prototype Places/People mode, recommendation reasons, value copy, follow success, Activity refresh, and the conditional place-recovery shelf.
- Test with five low-network users before production ranking infrastructure is built.
- Gate: at least four of five users can find someone worth following without coaching, and at least four can accurately explain what following changes.

### Slice 1: People acquisition loop

- Ship the two available graph sources and curated-real-profile fallback.
- Include search, recommendation dismissals, follow transitions, Activity empty/thin CTAs, and instrumentation.
- Do not include Contacts or device-location ranking.

### Slice 2: Place recovery loop

- Ship only after the provenance/copy prototype passes its trust gate.
- Candidate generation, dismissal, deduplication, and save resolution remain a separate flag and test cohort from People recommendations.
- Slice 1 can ship without Slice 2. Both are fully specified before engineering begins, as requested.
- Trust gate: test at least ten candidate cards spanning strong, weak, duplicate, and no-evidence conditions with five users. At least four of five must correctly explain why the strong shelf says “may have been,” all five must recognize that the weak shelf does not claim a visit, no more than one user may describe either surface as invasive or as evidence of background tracking, and all duplicate/no-evidence cases must avoid a false candidate. Any failed threshold blocks Slice 2 implementation and sends copy/provenance back to design review.

### Slice 3: source expansion, only after baseline data

- Consider native Contacts, stronger two-hop graph ranking, or profile-city expansion.
- Add one source at a time so conversion and dismiss quality remain attributable.

## Product Definitions

| Term | REC-90 definition |
|---|---|
| Low-network user | Authenticated user following 0–4 non-blocked accounts. |
| Established-network user | Authenticated user following 5 or more non-blocked accounts. |
| Empty Activity | Zero rows in the rendered privacy-visible followed-user Activity result after current filters. Older valid rows are rendered; there is no hidden 30-day cutoff. |
| Thin Activity | One or two rows in the rendered privacy-visible followed-user Activity result. |
| Populated Activity | Three or more rows in the rendered privacy-visible followed-user Activity result. |
| Active recommendation profile | Real, non-deleted, non-blocked profile with `recommendable = true`, at least one eligible source reason, at least one currently active `followers`-visible place, and an authoritative place-activity timestamp within 90 days. The timestamp is the REC-86 presentation timestamp: explicit `visited_at` when present, otherwise authoritative `saved_at`/creation fallback. It is used for eligibility/ranking only and is never returned or shown pre-follow. Curated fallback additionally requires `curated_fallback_eligible = true`, a non-empty avatar or initials, display name, handle, and bio. |
| Same city | Case- and diacritic-normalized equality between explicit profile-city values. Device location and precise coordinates are never used for this match. |
| Recent place evidence | Evidence captured within the last 30 days. |
| Strong place evidence | User explicitly started “I'm here right now” and received a resolved candidate, or intentionally imported a geotagged photo that resolved to a candidate, but did not finish saving it. |
| Weak place evidence | Search result open, map detail open, or manually started unresolved candidate without explicit presence evidence. |
| Durable profile dismissal | First dismissal creates a server-synchronized 90-day suggestion exclusion. A second dismissal of the same profile within 12 months creates a permanent suggestion exclusion. Explicit search remains allowed; the profile re-enters suggestions only after the viewer deliberately follows it or removes it from a future hidden-suggestions control. |
| Durable place dismissal | Device-local exclusion for 90 days for the merged candidate key. REC-90 does not claim cross-device place-candidate history. |

Graph-size analytics buckets are `0`, `1–4`, `5–9`, `10–24`, and `25+`, counted from server-confirmed, non-blocked following edges. Pending optimistic follows/unfollows do not change the analytics bucket until acknowledged. Low-network means buckets `0` and `1–4`; treatment availability is not limited to those buckets.

All authenticated users receive the same permanent Places/People structure and recommendation shelves. Low-network users are the initial prototype and experiment cohort, not a separate navigation variant. Empty/thin/populated Activity behavior is based only on rendered row count and applies to every authenticated user, regardless of graph-size bucket.

Activity remains newest-first by authoritative saved/visited timestamp under the REC-86 contract. Future-skew handling and stable tie-breaking are preserved.

## Visibility Contract

Before follow, a recommendation card may show only fields already allowed on the public profile shell:

- avatar or initials
- display name
- handle
- bio
- relationship-derived reason: Follows you or shared-follow count
- explicit profile city only when the owner has supplied it and the field is part of the approved public profile projection
- generic payoff copy: “Follow to see their shared places”

The authoritative existing shell is `ProfileShell` in `Wander/Services/RepositoryProtocols.swift`: `id`, `handle`, `displayName`, `avatarURL`, `bio`, and viewer-relative `relationship`. REC-90 adds no protected content to that shell. Profile city does not exist in the current shell; the city shelf requires a separate explicit, user-editable, public `discovery_city` projection plus disclosure in profile editing. If that migration and consent surface are not approved, Slice 1 omits the city shelf and uses More People to Follow. A private or device-inferred city is never substituted.

All proactive recommendation sources require explicit owner consent through `profiles.recommendable`, default `false` for existing and new profiles until the user opts in or a participating alpha tester records equivalent informed consent. The consent copy must disclose that rec.me may use the existence and recency—not the contents—of places shared with followers to decide whether and where to recommend the profile. Appearance in suggestions therefore permits a bounded inference that the person has shared at least one follower-visible place recently; that inference is explicitly approved only after privacy review of the consent and recommendation copy.

`profiles.curated_fallback_eligible` is a separate internal moderation/quality flag, default `false`. It cannot override `recommendable = false`. It controls only curated city/general fallback membership and has an auditable product/admin owner. `discovery_city` is separate user-provided public profile data and may be cleared at any time; clearing it removes city matching on the next response.

Before follow, REC-90 must not show place names, notes, coordinates, follower-visible place counts, inferred tastes, category aggregates, or recency derived from protected place rows.

After a successful one-way follow:

- The viewer can receive that owner's `followers`-visible places through the existing RLS-authorized visible-place contract.
- `mutuals`-visible places remain hidden unless the owner already follows the viewer, in which case the new edge creates a mutual relationship.
- Activity and place results refresh through the existing repository boundary.
- The card may confirm “Following” and “Their shared places are now in Discover” without claiming a specific unlocked count.

Already-followed profiles are excluded from proactive recommendation shelves. They remain eligible for explicit search results and render with a Following state. Current-user, blocked, deleted, unavailable, dismissed, and in-flight duplicate profiles are excluded from shelves.

## Recommendation Contract

### Shelf and source order

1. **Suggested for You:** follows-you candidates first, then one-hop graph expansion candidates.
2. **More in {Profile City}:** curated real profiles with a normalized explicit city match.
3. **More People to Follow:** curated real profiles when the user has no city or the city shelf has fewer than three eligible profiles.

There is no “Top Members” label in v1 because rec.me is not computing or endorsing a popularity rank.

### Deterministic reason precedence

If multiple reasons apply, show exactly one primary reason:

1. `Follows you`
2. `1 person you follow follows {displayNameOrHandle}` or `{N} people you follow follow {displayNameOrHandle}`
3. `Also in {city}`
4. `Suggested by rec.me`

`shared_follow_count` means the number of non-blocked accounts the viewer follows that also follow the candidate. It does not mean the viewer and candidate are mutual friends. The reason payload is calculated server-side after block/deletion filters. Count one uses singular grammar; count two or greater uses plural grammar; zero is never displayed. `{displayNameOrHandle}` uses the complete display name when non-empty and falls back to `@handle`; clients do not attempt culturally unsafe “first name” extraction. Copy is represented by reason kind plus count so it remains localization-ready. Sorting within a source is `shared_follow_count` descending, then eligible-profile activity timestamp descending, then normalized handle ascending for a stable tie-break. Activity timestamps influence ranking but are not shown pre-follow.

### Card fields

| Field | Source | Fallback | Privacy rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avatar | public profile shell | initials | Existing avatar projection and cache freshness contract. |
| Display name | public profile shell | handle | Required and truncated before CTA collision. |
| Handle | public profile shell | none | Required. |
| Bio | public profile shell | “Follow to see their shared places.” | Public shell only; two-line maximum. |
| Reason | recommendation response | `Suggested by rec.me` only for curated fallback | No private graph identities; only aggregate count. |
| City | explicit public profile field | omitted | Never derived from device coordinates. |
| Follow state | local store plus server relationship | loading state | Reconciled after mutation. |

### Recommendation response boundary

Existing profile search is not sufficient for proactive graph recommendations. Slice 1 requires a reviewed backend response that returns only:

- candidate public profile shell
- `source_kind`
- `reason_kind`
- optional aggregate `shared_follow_count`
- optional explicit profile city
- final server rank
- response generation timestamp

The response must exclude all place rows, place-derived counts/categories/recency, graph member identities, precise location, contact data, private notes, and blocked relationships. The server may use the existence and recency of an eligible `followers`-visible place as an eligibility/ranking predicate without returning that place-derived data. It must filter current user, existing follows, blocks in either direction, deleted/unavailable profiles, expired eligibility, and durable dismissals before returning data. Client filtering is defense in depth, not authorization.

The response is a shelf envelope, not an all-or-nothing array. Each shelf has independent `status` (`success`, `empty`, `error`), `items`, and coarse `error_category`, plus one top-level `generated_at`. A graph-shelf failure can therefore coexist with a valid curated-city shelf and vice versa.

Because an invoker path cannot safely inspect pre-follow `followers`-visible place eligibility, Slice 1 requires a narrow security-definer RPC or a separately maintained consented public eligibility projection. The recommended and default engineering path is the narrow security-definer RPC. It must return final rank only, pin `search_path`, grant only the authenticated role, scope the caller through `app.current_user_id()`, filter blocks/dismissals before ranking, and include metadata, grants, side-channel, and RLS regression tests under the repo's Supabase policy. Any alternative public projection requires a new plan-eng/privacy review before implementation.

### Search contract

- Search activates after two trimmed characters or immediately for an exact `@handle` submission.
- Debounce remote requests by 300 ms and cancel stale in-flight requests.
- Return at most 20 results in v1; pagination is deferred until observed demand.
- Rank exact handle, handle prefix, exact normalized display name, then normalized display-name prefix.
- Search results replace proactive shelves while a query is active. Clearing restores cached/refreshed shelves.
- Blocked, blocker, deleted, unavailable, and current-user profiles are removed server-side and again defensively client-side.
- An already-followed result is valid and renders Following; proactive shelves exclude it.
- A dismissed recommendation may still appear in explicit search because dismissal means “not in suggestions,” not “hide this person everywhere.”

## Place-Candidate Contract

The recovery surface is conditional, not an unconditional section with a misleading title.

| Evidence class | Section title | Allowed actions |
|---|---|---|
| At least one strong unresolved candidate | Places You May Have Been | Been, Wanna Go, open, dismiss |
| Weak unresolved candidates only | Pick Up Where You Left Off | Open, save through normal flow, dismiss |
| No eligible candidates | Section omitted | None |

Candidate identity uses the existing `VisiblePlaceGrouping` concept and precedence:

1. normalized provider + provider place ID
2. normalized canonical name + normalized address/locality/region
3. normalized canonical name + coordinate rounded to four decimal places, approximately 11 meters of latitude

Coordinate-only evidence is not a merge key and is not eligible for an automatic candidate card. It must first resolve to exactly one plausible provider/canonical place within 25 meters. If multiple venues are plausible, the evidence stays unresolved and the user enters normal candidate confirmation. This prevents colocated or vertically stacked venues from collapsing into one candidate.

Evidence enters the same alias set only under one of these deterministic rules:

- exact normalized provider plus provider place ID matches;
- normalized canonical name matches and normalized full address/locality/region matches;
- normalized canonical name matches, coordinates are within 25 meters, and neither record has a conflicting non-empty provider place ID;
- an existing alias bridge already proved identity under one of the rules above.

Different non-empty provider place IDs never merge solely because names or coordinates are close. Evidence that resolves to a valid overlapping alias set merges into one candidate. The merged candidate keeps the strongest evidence class, newest evidence timestamp, and all aliases. A candidate matching an existing current-user save is removed rather than offered again. A successful Been/Wanna Go action enters the normal save flow and then removes every alias from the shelf. A duplicate save response is treated as resolved, not as an error card.

Place evidence and dismissals are device-local in REC-90 v1. They are not uploaded merely to populate this shelf. A resolved user place follows the normal server sync contract. Device-local evidence expires after 30 days; dismissals expire after 90 days. This is an intentional privacy and feasibility boundary, and the UI must not promise cross-device candidate continuity.

Analytics uses a random device-local candidate analytics UUID, not a plain hash of provider ID, name, address, or coordinates. The UUID is stored with the local merged candidate, rotates when the 30-day evidence lifecycle expires, and is never sent with raw place identity in the same event.

### Mutation authority

- Follow/unfollow: server-authoritative; optimistic local state with rollback and existing offline-queue rules only if the repository already guarantees idempotency.
- Profile recommendation dismissal: server-authoritative for signed-in users, optimistic locally, queued/retried idempotently with `(viewer_id, profile_id, surface)` uniqueness.
- Place evidence and place dismissal: device-local and privacy-preserving until the user explicitly resolves a place.
- Been/Wanna Go resolution: normal user-place save contract; server-authoritative after sync, local-first while pending.
- Block changes: existing hard-block contract wins immediately and evicts affected cards, searches, profiles, activity, and cached details.

### Cache truthfulness

- Online recommendation envelopes expire after 15 minutes and refresh on foreground entry to People.
- Offline curated public-shell cards may render from cache for at most 24 hours with “Saved earlier”; mutations remain disabled until online reconciliation.
- Graph-derived cards and relationship reasons (`Follows you`, shared-follow count) are hidden once their 15-minute cache age expires. They are not downgraded into a generic claim because the candidate itself may no longer be eligible.
- Server responses guarantee block/deletion/dismissal filtering at generation time. Offline UI can guarantee only locally known state, so the zero-leak guardrail is scoped accordingly. Any locally known block/deletion/dismissal evicts immediately regardless of cache age.
- On reconnect, reconcile blocks/relationships before enabling Follow or opening a cached recommended profile.

## Constraints

### Product

- Preserve rec.me as a map-first, place-first utility, not a public influencer feed.
- Following remains one-way; friends remain mutual follows.
- There is no global people directory, leaderboard, popularity rank, or “top creator” mechanic.
- Recommendations must state one approved v1 reason: “Follows you,” “1 person you follow follows Maya,” “2 people you follow follow Maya,” “Also in Los Angeles,” or “Suggested by rec.me.”
- A person card must explain the value of following; avatar and name alone are insufficient. Before follow, v1 may show only public profile fields, graph reason, and “Follow to see their shared places.” It must not preview private place names, notes, coordinates, or counts.
- Blocked, deleted, unavailable, current-user, and already-dismissed profiles must not leak into recommendation shelves.
- Following never grants more than Supabase RLS allows. Client previews are privacy-filtered.
- Fictional production accounts and fabricated social proof are prohibited.

### Places You May Have Been

- rec.me must not imply continuous background location tracking unless that capability is separately designed, consented to, disclosed, and implemented.
- v1 candidates must come from honest first-party evidence available to the app, such as foreground current-location candidate resolution, recent place searches/opens, unresolved add candidates, or photo location metadata the user intentionally supplied.
- Weak evidence must use weaker copy. If rec.me only knows that a place was recently viewed, the section should be titled **Pick Up Where You Left Off**, not **Places You May Have Been**.
- Strong-evidence candidates can be marked Been or Wanna Go, opened, or dismissed. Weak-evidence candidates can only be opened, saved through the normal confirmation flow, or dismissed.
- Dismissal is durable for the candidate/evidence window and must not immediately resurrect after refresh.
- A saved or already-resolved place is removed or converted to a resolved state without creating duplicates.

### Technical and operational

- Native SwiftUI, iOS 17+, iPhone-first, Dynamic Type and VoiceOver compliant.
- Reuse repository/protocol boundaries. Views must not call Clerk or Supabase directly.
- Supabase RLS remains authoritative for social visibility and block behavior.
- Local SwiftData/cache behavior remains honest while offline.
- No auth, payment, or database policy changes occur without explicit implementation review.
- The current assignment stops after product, full-state design, and engineering specifications. It does not seed production data or implement UI/backend changes.
- Any eventual app-code release follows the explicit PR, validation, and TestFlight workflow; planning approval alone does not ship a build.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Beli-balanced

Places opens by default with Activity followed by a conditional, provenance-appropriate place-recovery shelf. People is a peer mode with search, proactive suggestions, explicit-profile-city relevance, and follow actions.

Pros:

- Matches Joe's stated direction and the supplied references.
- Preserves rec.me's place-first identity.
- Builds on the existing Places/Members implementation rather than changing navigation.
- Separates place recovery from people acquisition without creating another tab.

Risks:

- People can remain undiscovered if users never switch modes.
- A long Places screen can bury the memory-recovery shelf.
- Curated city suggestions can drift into stranger/influencer discovery without strict eligibility and copy guardrails.

### Approach B: Follow-first onboarding state

Users below a graph-size threshold open People first until they follow enough relevant accounts; established users open Places first.

Pros:

- Directly attacks network cold start.
- Makes the primary growth action unavoidable.

Risks:

- Violates the expectation that Discover answers a place need.
- Creates threshold-dependent navigation and test complexity.
- Can feel like forced social onboarding rather than utility.

### Approach C: Contextual social recommendations

Places remains the only primary mode. Person recommendations are inserted between Activity and place recovery, and profiles are discovered through place attribution.

Pros:

- Every person recommendation has place context.
- Fewer top-level modes and less empty social chrome.

Risks:

- Mixes two user jobs and makes the main feed harder to scan.
- Reduces space for member search and connection-specific explanations.
- Risks becoming a generic feed, which the product explicitly rejects.

## Recommended Approach

Proceed with **Approach A: Beli-balanced**, selected by Joe.

The product should copy Beli's clarity, not its restaurant-only or popularity-oriented model. Places remains the default because rec.me is a place utility. People becomes a deliberate acquisition surface whose cards answer two questions before asking for a follow:

1. Why am I seeing this person?
2. What access changes when I follow them?

The causal loop is:

```text
Open Discover
  -> see thin/empty followed activity
  -> switch to People or tap Find People
  -> understand recommendation reason and place value
  -> follow a real account
  -> activity and visible place graph refresh
  -> return to Places with a richer result set
```

Places You May Have Been is a separate supply loop:

```text
App receives honest foreground evidence
  -> proposes an unresolved candidate with provenance-appropriate copy
  -> user marks Been, Wanna Go, dismisses, or opens details
  -> resolved candidate leaves the shelf
  -> saved place appears on the user's map under normal privacy rules
```

## Resolved V1 Decisions

- The may-have-been title requires strong, user-initiated foreground evidence; weak evidence uses Pick Up Where You Left Off.
- People geography uses explicit profile city only. Device location is not a recommendation input and Discover does not trigger a location prompt.
- Pre-follow cards show public profile context and access-explanation copy, not protected place previews or aggregates.
- A successful follow remains in place as Following for the session, then disappears from proactive shelves on the next full refresh. It remains available in search.
- Every proactive candidate requires user-facing `recommendable = true` consent and eligible follower-visible activity within 90 days. Curated fallback profiles additionally require complete public shell fields and internal `curated_fallback_eligible = true`.
- The internal curated flag is owned by the rec.me product/admin team; disabling either flag removes the profile from new recommendation responses immediately and from cached shelves on refresh/reconciliation.

## Success Criteria

Because there is no baseline demand evidence, the first release is successful only if behavior and trust both improve.

### Primary follow outcome

- Prototype gate: at least four of five low-network testers find one person worth following without coaching; at least four correctly explain the access change; at least three complete a follow.
- Alpha directional gate: at least 50% of the first ten eligible, exposed low-network testers follow one recommended person within their first two exposed Discover sessions.
- Median time from first settled recommendation-shelf impression to first recommendation-attributed follow is under 60 seconds for converting testers.
- Production target, activated only after a usable baseline exists: increase unique exposed-user follow conversion by at least 25% relative to the baseline/holdout contract below.

### Value comprehension

- In moderated alpha testing, at least 8 of 10 testers can explain why a suggested person was shown and what following changes.
- Exploratory only: compare recommendation-card and search-result conversion without claiming causality. A causal copy/card treatment claim requires randomized variants within the same recommendation surface and candidate pool.

### Place recovery

- At least 20% of strong-evidence place candidates receive a positive supply action: Been or Wanna Go.
- Report durable dismiss rate separately as candidate-quality/trust evidence; dismissal never counts as place-supply success.
- Fewer than 10% of tested candidates are described by testers as invasive, inexplicable, or falsely implying location tracking.

### Guardrails

- Zero blocked/private/deleted/current-user profiles appear in freshly generated server payloads or remain after the client knows that state. Offline caches obey the explicit expiry/reconciliation contract and never claim stronger freshness.
- Zero private place details are shown beyond the viewer's pre-follow RLS access.
- Zero production fictional accounts or fabricated social proof.
- Follow, unfollow, dismiss, save, offline, loading, and retry states are observable and idempotent.

### Required instrumentation

- `discover_viewed`: `mode`, `graph_size_bucket`, `activity_state`, `is_offline`, `build`.
- `discover_mode_switched`: `from_mode`, `to_mode`, `entry_point` (`tab`, `activity_cta`, `empty_cta`).
- `discover_recommendation_shelf_viewed`: `shelf_kind`, `eligible_card_count`, `graph_size_bucket`, `cache_state`.
- `discover_recommendation_card_viewed`: `candidate_id`, `shelf_kind`, `source_kind`, `reason_kind`, `position`; never profile copy or private payloads.
- `discover_recommendation_action`: `candidate_id`, `action` (`open_profile`, `dismiss`, `undo_dismiss`, `follow_attempt`, `follow_success`, `follow_failure`), `source_kind`, `position`, `error_category` when relevant.
- `discover_people_search`: `query_length_bucket`, `result_count_bucket`, `outcome` (`success`, `empty`, `error`, `cancelled`); never raw query.
- `discover_place_candidate_viewed`: `candidate_analytics_id`, `evidence_class`, `position`, `age_bucket`.
- `discover_place_candidate_action`: `candidate_analytics_id`, `evidence_class`, `action` (`open`, `been`, `wanna_go`, `dismiss`, `undo_dismiss`, `save_success`, `save_failure`), `error_category` when relevant.
- `discover_graph_changed`: `direction` (`follow`, `unfollow`), `source_surface`, `graph_size_bucket_before`, `graph_size_bucket_after`.

A shelf impression counts once per screen appearance when at least 50% of the shelf container is visible for one continuous second and the request is settled. A card impression uses the same rule per candidate ID per screen appearance. Follow success emits only after server acknowledgement; optimistic UI does not count as conversion. Retry attempts emit attempts separately but only one success per resulting edge.

### Experiment denominator and rollout

- Eligible cohort: authenticated low-network users who open Discover and are not staff/test fixture identities.
- Exposure: a settled People shelf with at least one eligible recommendation meets the impression rule above.
- Primary denominator: unique eligible exposed users, not sessions or card impressions.
- Conversion window: a recommendation-attributed successful follow within 24 hours of the user's first exposure in a seven-day measurement window.
- Repeat sessions: one user contributes at most once to the denominator and once to conversion per seven-day window.
- Search-attributed follows are reported separately from proactive-shelf follows.
- Baseline: collect at least seven days and 30 eligible Discover users under the current search-only Members experience before claiming a lift. If traffic cannot reach 30, report qualitative alpha outcomes and raw counts without statistical language.
- Holdout: once at least 40 eligible users can be allocated, assignment may begin deterministically 50/50 by stable internal ID to treatment or existing Members control. Forty users is an operational start threshold, not an efficacy sample. Before claiming a 25% relative lift, calculate the required sample from the observed baseline conversion using two-sided alpha 0.05 and 80% power; keep assignment stable across devices and sessions until that powered sample or a predeclared time limit is reached. Below that threshold, report raw counts and directional intervals only.
- Slice 2 place recovery uses a separate feature flag, exposure event, cohort, and success report. Its outcomes are never credited to the follow experiment.

Analytics must remain non-PII: internal IDs, coarse source categories, counts, state, build, and timestamps only.
